The short version

Your mineral is a machine you hold, in the EU, running your assistant on your own Claude account. Your brain lives on it as ordinary files. Nobody at Crads AI reads your notes. That is a description of how it is built, not a promise about our conduct: your pages sit on your box, and operating the box does not include reading it.

What we can see is operational: whether your mineral is alive, which software it runs, and whatever you have deliberately switched on for your community. The rest of this page is that sentence, in detail.

Where your mineral runs

On a virtual machine rented from Hetzner Online GmbH, in the EU only: their data centres in Nuremberg or Falkenstein (Germany) or Helsinki (Finland). Which one depends on capacity at the moment your mineral was built. No mineral is provisioned outside the EU.

If you are in Australia, as most of the beta is, that means your data is stored overseas. We are telling you so plainly because Australian privacy law expects us to, and because you should know it regardless.

What lives on your mineral, and stays there

  • Your brain. The pages your assistant writes and reads: who you are, your

goals, your people, your business, your voice.

  • Your credentials. Your Claude sign-in, your connected service tokens, your

Telegram bot token if you use one.

  • Your run history. What your assistant did and when.

None of this is copied to us. There is no central store of member brains, because there is no central store: one member, one machine.

What leaves your mineral

Three things, and nothing else.

1. A heartbeat, to your community

If your mineral belongs to a rock, it sends that rock a small status record. What it contains is fixed in the software, and two switches under Privacy and access control most of it:

Always sentSent only if you enable "activity"Sent only if you enable "engagement"
when it was generatedwhether onboarding is donewhich skills are installed
whether Claude is signed inhow full the disk iswhich skills are enabled

No page, no note, no message, no email, no calendar entry, and no name from your brain is in that record. Turn both switches off and your rock sees only that your mineral exists, is reachable, and is signed in.

2. A backup, to a repository you own

Your mineral takes a nightly encrypted snapshot of the parts of it that are not already in your own git repository. It is encrypted on your box with AES-256 and a passphrase generated on your box, then pushed to your own private GitHub repository. We never hold the passphrase and the repository is not ours. If you have not connected a repository, the snapshot stays local.

3. Registration pointers, to the directory

A small Cloudflare Worker routes people to the right mineral and records who holds what. It stores:

  • a one-way hash of your email address, not the address itself
  • which minerals your account holds, and your role on each
  • which communities you belong to, and whether that link is active

It holds no credentials, no brain content and no message content. We do not treat the email hash as anonymous, because a hash of a known email can be recognised; we treat it as personal information and this section discloses it as such.

Services you connect

  • Google (Gmail, Calendar, Drive). You create your own Google OAuth client

and it stays on your mineral. Your Google key never touches Crads AI infrastructure, and we could not read your mail if we wanted to. The trade is a guided one-time setup and a weekly re-sign-in, which is a consequence of using your own key rather than an oversight.

  • Microsoft 365. Different, and worth knowing: this one uses a single Crads AI

application registration, so our application name appears on your consent screen and we are in that authorisation path. The tokens still live on your mineral and we do not receive your mail.

  • Everything else in the connections catalogue signs in on your mineral, with

the token stored on your mineral.

  • Anthropic. Your assistant runs on your Claude account, under your

agreement with Anthropic. What you send your assistant, you are sending through their service on your own account.

When we can reach your mineral

Two ways, both bounded.

Software updates. Your mineral restarts nightly onto the software your community has published. That updates the machinery, not your files, and you can switch it off.

Support access, only if you grant it. If something is broken and you want us to look, you grant access explicitly. That grant expires on a clock, is recorded on your own mineral, and every use of it is logged where you can read it. We cannot add ourselves to your mineral later without you granting it.

The key your mineral is born with

One honest exception, and version 0.2 of this page got it wrong by saying there was none.

If your mineral was built for you by a community, somebody had to build the machine before you could reach it, and that was us. So the machine is born with a Crads AI setup key on it. It is not hidden: it appears in your own list of machines, named "Crads-AI setup access (not your computer)", and it has a Remove button like any other.

What happens to it:

  • It is removed when your mineral is handed to you. The handover revokes it

and then checks it is actually gone, and says so loudly if it is not.

  • You can remove it yourself, at any time, from that same list. You do not

need our permission and we are not told first.

  • We cannot put it back. Adding a machine to your mineral needs your

approval, which is the same gate that stops anyone else adding one.

Two things worth knowing while it is there. Until handover completes, that key opens your mineral, so if you can see it on your list after you have taken ownership, remove it and tell us. And if you built your mineral yourself rather than receiving one from a community, your own key is the first one on it and this section does not apply to you at all.

What a support session can see

Worth being exact about, because "we can look at your mineral" and "we can read your saved passwords" are different sentences and you should know which one you are agreeing to.

Your mineral stores secrets in two tiers, and the app labels which tier each one is in:

  • Available to the box (the default for anything a scheduled job needs at

3am). Stored as a value on your disk. Encrypting it there would be theatre, because the box would have to keep the key on the same disk to use it at 3am. Anyone with access to the server can read these, and that includes us during a support session you have granted.

  • Sealed to your devices. Stored as an envelope only your own enrolled

machines can open. There is deliberately no code on your mineral that can decrypt these, so a support session cannot read them, and neither can a scheduled job.

If a credential would be damaging in someone else's hands and nothing scheduled needs it, put it in the sealed tier. If you have granted a support session and change your mind, revoke it: the grant is a record on your own mineral, not a key we hold.

The same is true of your brain during a support session. A granted session is server access, and server access can read files. We do not, that is not what support is for, and every use of the grant is logged where you can see it. But the honest statement is that the protection during an active grant is the log and the clock, not an inability.

What your community can and cannot see

Your rock hosts your mineral. It cannot read your brain, your mail, your messages or your notes. It sees the heartbeat described above, whatever you have chosen to share, and the fact of your membership. This is structural: your pages are on your machine, and hosting a machine does not grant reading it.

Taking your data, and deletion

Your brain is a folder of readable files in a git repository. There is no export step, because there is nothing to export from: it is already yours, in a portable format, on a machine you hold. You can take the box itself, keep the repository, or both.

Ask us to delete your mineral and we destroy the machine and its disk. Copies you hold, including your own backup repository, remain yours and we cannot reach them.

What the directory keeps afterwards, stated honestly. Version 0.3 of this page said deletion removes your registration pointers. That is only partly true today, and the honest version is:

Removed when your mineral is destroyed:

  • its route, so the address stops resolving anywhere
  • its membership links to any community
  • its build record

Also removed, as of 24 August 2026:

  • the machine's registration record, holding its address, a one-way hash of the

owner's email, and a hash of the access token it authenticated with

  • the record of where the mineral came from
  • the pointer to it in the owner's own list of machines

Still expiring on a timer rather than being deleted outright:

  • the mineral's own directory entry

None of that is your brain, your notes or your content: those live on the disk we destroy. It is bookkeeping about a machine that no longer exists.

We found the gap while checking this page against the software rather than against our intentions. Two of those records already expired on their own after 400 days, so nothing was kept forever, but 400 days is not what "we deleted your mineral" should mean. They now go when the machine goes.

Breaches

If member data is exposed, we will tell affected members what happened, what was exposed, and what to do, without waiting to have a complete picture first. Given what is stored where, the realistic exposure is the directory's registration pointers rather than brain content.
