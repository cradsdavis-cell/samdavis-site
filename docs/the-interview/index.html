Getting set up

The interview

This is the part that makes the difference between an assistant and a chatbot with your logo on it, and it is the part people put off.

A new mineral knows nothing. The interview is how it comes to know your world: what you are trying to do, who is in it, how you work, what to never touch. It takes about an hour, it does not have to happen in one sitting, and every other page in these docs assumes it has happened.

Starting it#

Open the Terminal on your mineral (the bottom entry in the sidebar; the page is titled Meet your assistant), type this, and press Enter:

/onboard

That is the whole ceremony. If you are partway through from an earlier sitting, the same command resumes it; you never have to remember where you were.

The first question is what to call it#

Before anything else, it asks what you want to call it, and writes that down immediately rather than at the end.

That is deliberate. Naming something you are about to talk to for an hour is a natural thing to be asked. Naming it in a setup form is naming a server, which is a colder and different act. And because it is written on the first turn, someone who walks away mid-interview comes back to something with a name rather than to "your assistant".

One name, everywhere. The mineral and the assistant share it: the sidebar, your seat page, the network map and the greeting all say the same word, and the Rename button changes that one thing. Renaming never touches the address, the connection or the keys.

If your mineral already has a name, it will not ask. If you have no preference, it keeps the one it has. It will not invent one for you.

The eight layers#

The interview walks eight layers, in order, and does not move on until the current one is genuinely covered rather than merely touched.

LayerWhat it is really asking
1North starthe long-term why, and what winning looks like in five to ten years
2Philosophyyour values, what you optimise for, what you will not compromise, and how you decide
3Selfyour story, what energises and drains you, how you handle pressure, and your actual work: what you offer, to whom, at what price
4Networkthe people. Customers, suppliers, team, mentors, family. Who they are, what they need, who owes whom, what is going cold
5Pastwhat you have built, the war stories, the lessons
6Goalsthis quarter and this year, with success criteria, and your anti-goals
7Taskswhat you are actually working on right now
8Workflowyour rhythm, how you like to be nudged, what to handle and what to never touch, your tools, how you write and speak, and your hard limits

Layer 4 becomes a page per person. Layer 8 captures real samples of your writing, which is what lets your assistant draft as you rather than as a language model.

How it actually feels#

One question per turn. Not a form, not a wall of questions. It asks, you answer, it follows the thread you opened rather than reading down a checklist.

It probes the thin bits. If an answer skims a layer, the next question goes at the thinnest point rather than moving on politely.

It stops when a layer is covered, not when the questions run out.

You can stop whenever you like#

There is no pause button because none is needed: just stop answering, close the window if you like. Progress is written to your mineral after every single turn, so nothing you have said is ever lost, and typing /onboard next time resumes exactly where it stopped, days later if need be.

That is the honest way to do it: an hour is a lot to find in one piece, and three twenty-minute sittings produce a better brain than one exhausted hour. The Onboarding card on your Overview counts the layers as they land, so you can always see how far in you are.

What happens at the end#

Two passes, not one.

Synthesis. Your answers become proper pages: written up, cross-linked, with a page per person you mentioned. Not a transcript. Your verbatim answers are kept separately during capture, and the writing-up happens afterwards so nothing is pre-polished into blandness while you are still talking.

Review. It walks you back through each layer in plain language: here is what I captured about your north star, is that right? You correct it, and the corrections stick.

Only then is the brain seeded. You can see the result on the Brain page, and the count on your Overview reads eight of eight.

If your community already answered some of it#

If your rock has published its own context to you, the interview reads it first and skips what your community already establishes, telling you so as it goes.

Your brain then points at that shared context rather than copying it, which means when your community updates it, you get the update instead of a stale copy.

If you are setting up a rock#

The same eight layers, asked about the community instead of about a person: what it is for, what it stands for, who is in it, what it promises its members, how it operates.

Do this before you invite anybody. A rock cannot build minerals for its members until its own brain is onboarded: the check that gates creating a member reads exactly this. It is not a formality, either. Everything your members' assistants inherit about your community comes from these answers.

What it will not do#

It will not invent things about you. If something is undecided, it records that it is undecided rather than filling the gap with something plausible.

It will not surface the private things casually. Layers 3 and 8 collect real context: health, pressure, hard boundaries. That is stored and flagged, not sprinkled into a morning brief.

It will not finish without you. The review pass needs your corrections. A brain nobody checked is a brain nobody should trust.
