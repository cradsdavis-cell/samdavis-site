What your community can see

What leaves your mineral, and who receives it.

If your mineral belongs to a rock, it tells that rock some things about itself. This page is the whole list, because "we respect your privacy" is not an answer and a list is.

The list#

Your mineral sends a small status record to its rock. Three groups:

Always sent, if you are in a community at all

FieldWhat it means
when it was generatedso a stale record is visibly stale
whether Claude is signed inan unsigned mineral cannot do anything, and your host can help
which software build it runsso your host knows if you are behind
which skills came from your rockwhat they gave you, reflected back
any catalogue requestsskills you asked your rock for
your two sharing choicesso "withheld" reads as withheld, not as zero
machinery run outcomeswhether the jobs that keep your mineral healthy ran, never what your own skills did

Sent unless you switch off "activity"

FieldWhat it means
whether onboarding is donewhether the interview finished
how full the disk isso somebody notices before it fills
when you were last activea rough liveness signal

Sent unless you switch off "engagement"

FieldWhat it means
which skills are installedthe list, not the contents
which are enabled on a scheduleditto
when skills ran, and which succeededtimestamps and outcomes

What is not in it, at all#

No page. No note. No sentence from your brain. No email, no message, no calendar entry, no contact, no name, no file. Not redacted, not summarised: not collected. There is no field in the record that could carry it.

Your mineral also writes a much fuller version of that status file for itself, locally, so you can see what it knows. That one is never sent anywhere. The two files sit next to each other on your disk with different names, which is a slightly graceless design that makes the boundary impossible to blur.

Why the switches default the way they do#

Both start on, and each is yours to switch off, any time, without asking anyone. That default is a real choice we made, so here is the reasoning, honestly, and you can disagree with it by flipping two switches.

A host who can see that eleven of twelve minerals ran their morning brief and one has not run anything for nine days can go and ask that person if they are stuck. A host who can see none of that is running a community blind, and the person who is stuck stays stuck. Communities work when hosts can host; a member who would rather be unobserved than helped is one switch away from exactly that, and the record even tells the host it is your choice rather than a fault.

What a host cannot do, structurally#

A host cannot read your brain. Not "does not": cannot. Your pages are files on your machine, and hosting a machine does not grant reading it. There is no console we are choosing not to build.

The exception you should know about is not the host, it is us: if you grant a support session, that is server access, and server access can read files. The privacy policy sets out what that means and what to do about the credentials you care about.

Changing your mind#

The switches are on the sharing page and take effect on the next heartbeat, which is minutes. Turning one off stops the flow from then on; it does not retract what a host already saw, in the same way that closing a curtain does not unsee. If that matters to you, switch off early rather than late.
