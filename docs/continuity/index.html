What happens if Crads AI goes away

The first serious objection I got to this whole thing was not about the software. It was: what happens if you pick up another job, or decide this isn't something you want to do any more.

It is the right question, it was asked by someone whose members would have been the ones affected, and the answer cannot be "don't worry". So here it is properly.

What I am

One person. Samuel Davis, trading as Crads AI, in Sydney. Not a company with a board and a runway, and I am not going to dress it up as one. That is the actual risk you are weighing, and no architecture makes a sole operator into an institution.

What I can do is make sure the risk is survivable rather than fatal, which is a different and achievable thing. Most software you buy fails badly when the vendor disappears, because the vendor holds the thing you actually needed. This is built so that I do not.

Three reasons this survives me

Your brain is files, not a database row. Your pages are markdown in a git repository, on a machine you hold, mirrored to a repository in your own GitHub account. If Crads AI stopped existing tonight, you would still have every page, readable in any text editor, on infrastructure I have no access to. There is no proprietary format and no export button, because there is nothing to export from.

You can take the box. Ownership is a pointer that can move. A member can hold their own mineral outright, a community can hand a mineral to the member on it, and a member can walk away with it. That mechanism exists today and works in both directions; it is not a promise about a future version. If I vanished, the machine would keep running the software it already has, and you would keep the keys to it.

Nothing routes through me at runtime. Your assistant is your Claude account talking to your files on your machine. It does not call a Crads AI API to think. If my infrastructure went dark, your mineral would keep working: what you would lose is new software, the community directory, and me.

What you would actually lose

I would rather be specific than reassuring.

  • Updates stop. Your mineral keeps running the last build it received. Over

months that matters: connectors break when services change their APIs, and nobody would be fixing them.

  • The directory goes quiet. Joining, invitations, transfers and the community

listing all run through a small service I operate. Existing minerals keep working; new links between them stop being made.

  • Support ends. Anything on your box that breaks in a way you cannot fix

yourself stays broken, unless you or someone you hire picks it up. It is ordinary Linux, node and markdown, so that is a real option rather than a theoretical one.

  • Your rock's shared skills stop arriving. What is already installed stays.

What I will do if I stop

Committing to this in writing is the only part of this page that is a promise rather than a description:

  1. Notice, not silence. If I wind this down deliberately, every member gets

told with a stated date, not left to notice the updates stopped.

  1. Ownership handed over first. Before anything is switched off, every mineral

is transferred outright to the person or community holding it, and every backup repository is confirmed as theirs and working.

  1. The software goes with you. The code that runs your mineral gets handed to

you, or opened, so that anyone you hire can keep it running.

  1. Backups verified before the lights go off. A backup nobody has tested is

not a backup. Restores get checked before a wind-down completes, not after.

What I cannot promise

I cannot promise the business continues. I cannot promise that if I am hit by a bus tomorrow, the orderly version above happens rather than the abrupt one: it is one person and there is no second operator today. That is why the first three sections matter more than the fourth. The design is the guarantee; my intentions are only the improvement on it.

If you are a community leader deciding whether to put your name on this for your members, that is the trade in one sentence: the operator is a single point of failure, and the thing your members depend on is deliberately not.
