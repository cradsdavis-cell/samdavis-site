Your pebble

Who owns this mineral, where its backup goes, and anything waiting on your answer.

The page about the machine itself rather than about what it knows. Most people open it twice: once out of curiosity, and once when something about ownership or backup needs settling.

The page names itself after your mineral#

"Your pebble" is only the placeholder. The moment the first read from your mineral lands, the heading becomes the mineral's name, which is also your assistant's name: one name, everywhere. The word "pebble" demotes to the subtitle line, where it belongs, because it describes the kind of thing this is, not which one is yours. Only the sidebar tab keeps the tier word, since it names the page rather than the mineral.

Rename sits beside the name, and the form under it says exactly how far a rename reaches: "Just the name you see. The address and your keys stay exactly as they are." Renaming changes what you and your assistant are called, and nothing else.

What it tells you#

Who holds it. The full custody model, four separate answers, is set out in who edits what. This page shows three of them, as three dials; the fourth, who may read it, lives on the Sharing page, because reading is a consent you manage there, not a property of the machine.

Where the backup goes. Whether a repository is connected, and whether the snapshot is recoverable. A backup nobody can decrypt is reported as not recoverable, which is the honest state rather than a comforting one. See back up your mineral.

Anything waiting on you. Transfers of custody, and consent your rock has asked for. Nothing in this list happens without you: a request sits here until you answer it. (Support access is separate: you grant that yourself, from the Sharing page, and nothing about it appears here.)

The three dials#

  • Ownership: "Yours" ("This mineral is yours, and it goes with you"), or the rock's name ("A work asset the rock owns").
  • Anchor: the rock that looks after this mineral, by name, or "None" ("No rock manages this mineral").
  • Hosting: "Your metal" if it runs on your own machine, or "Crads AI" ("Crads AI supplies the machine underneath").

Three independent answers, and every combination is legitimate: a mineral you own can be anchored to a rock and hosted by Crads AI, or none of the above.

Backup#

The Backup card has two renderings. Connected, it shows the repository by name, when the last snapshot was sent, and whose account it lives in ("private, in your own GitHub account, every night" for a mineral you own; an org-owned mineral says plainly that the repository is in the rock's account instead). Not connected, it does not soften anything: "Your brain lives on this mineral and nowhere else."

Download a copy now works in both states, no GitHub account needed. It hands you the brain as a file, straight from the mineral. So even before you connect a repository, a copy of everything is one click away.

Connecting GitHub without leaving the page#

Connect my GitHub runs the whole flow inside the card. Press it and you get a short code and a github.com address to enter it at, on any device. "Waiting for you to approve it. This card checks by itself." Once you approve, the card walks through its steps line by line, and finishes with where your brain now lives: a private repository, yours, which the mineral keeps up to date from here.

If a step fails, the card names the leg that failed and keeps the lines that did work on screen, so a retry is an informed decision rather than a guess: "Nothing was lost: every step is safe to run again, so pressing Connect my GitHub picks up where this stopped."

Waiting on you#

Five kinds of row can appear here, and the empty state is "Nothing needs your answer."

  • A rock transfer. A rock has staged a transfer of this mineral. "Nothing moves until you accept it here."
  • An ownership grant. Ownership has been granted to you; taking it makes a copy of the brain under your own GitHub account, which is the same flow as the Backup card runs.
  • An ask to read your pages. "Your rock asks to always be able to read your brain pages." What accepting grants is spelled out on the row: until you turn it off, they may read your brain pages, never your secrets. And declining is not a dropped request: it is recorded as a real no.
  • An ask to install skills. Standing consent to push skills and updates onto this mineral without asking each time. Declining is recorded as a real no; they can ask again later.
  • A framework offer. Accepting installs the rock's material on your mineral under their own name. Your own pages are never edited.

Each row shows when it was asked and any note the rock attached, and carries its own Accept and Decline. Underneath, one rule holds all of this together: a grant is about reading, never about management, and it is your mineral that records the answer. The rock that manages you can never quietly grant itself a way in.

Accepting a transfer signs you in first#

A bare accept is refused, by design. Pressing Accept on a transfer first signs you in, bound to this specific request, records your consent at the directory, and gets a receipt; only then does the mineral act, carrying that receipt. Your sign-in is the consent, which is what makes a custody change attributable to a person rather than to whoever had the tab open.

Becoming a rock#

The "Your rock" card is the way to become a rock, and it renders one of five states:

  • Already a rock: "This mineral is a rock." Others can join it, and you still have this seat.
  • Blocked by ownership: a rock has to own itself, so while a rock owns this mineral it cannot become one. The card names the owner and the fix: ask them to hand the mineral over to you first.
  • The offer and the form: turn this mineral into a rock others can join, same address, same key, same seat. One thing changes hands, and it is the big one: your personal brain becomes the rock's brain. A verified copy is pushed to your own repository first. You type the consent sentence to arm the button.
  • In flight: "This mineral is becoming a rock." You can close the app; it finishes on its own, and the card picks it up again whenever you come back, from the mineral's own record rather than the tab that started it.
  • Stopped: a promotion that failed part-way says so, confirms your mineral is untouched and your backup is still yours, and offers "Clear it and start over".

The full walkthrough, including what happens to an anchor you already have, lives at become a rock.

Origin#

The Origin fold answers where this mineral came from, once, permanently. A mineral a rock seeded shows who seeded it and the framework imprint it was born with. One that was not shows "Self-started. Nobody seeded this mineral." Either way the record is frozen: who seeded this mineral never changes, whatever moves later. Ownership can transfer, anchors can come and go; lineage is history, and history does not get edited.

Why there is no danger zone#

There is no delete button on this page, and that is not an oversight to hunt past. Deleting a pebble is not self-serve: an org-owned mineral is the rock's to end, and a self-hosted one is removed by removing the install. Rather than show a danger zone with nothing live in it, the page shows none at all.

The part worth knowing before you need it#

Custody can move in both directions, and every direction needs the sign-in of the person receiving it. A community can hand a mineral to the member on it, and a member can accept one that was anchored to a community.

Nothing flips behind anyone's back. A change of custody is recorded with a receipt at the moment you sign in to accept it, and every support grant and removal is logged on the Sharing page, under Support access, where you granted it.
