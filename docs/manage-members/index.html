How to

Invite and manage members

Everything a host does with members happens on the Members page. (New to the words rock and pebble? Rocks and pebbles defines them once, plainly.)

The two kinds of membership#

Worth getting straight before you invite anyone, because it decides who pays and what happens if the arrangement ends.

JoinedAnchored
Whose machinetheirs, they brought itone you had built for them
Who pays for hostingthemyou
If they leavethey keep it and walk awaythey keep it and walk away
Counts toward your seatsyesyes

The last row is the one people expect to differ, and it does not. A member keeps their mineral either way. An anchored member whose host stops paying does not lose their brain; the arrangement about hosting ends, the mineral does not.

Paying for someone's box buys them hosting. It does not buy you their data, and it does not buy you control of their mineral. The hosting agreement says so in as many words.

Building a mineral for someone, step by step#

This is the anchored path: you build it, you pay for its hosting, they own it. Two prerequisites, both checked for you: your rock has GitHub connected, and your rock's own interview is done. If either is missing, the page says so instead of building something half-briefed.

  1. On Members, press + New pebble at the top. A form unfolds; the summary line on it is honest about the size of this: two questions and it builds.
  2. Whose pebble will this be? Two cards, and the choice matters more than any other setting: Theirs is a private assistant, a brain of its own. It belongs to them and goes with them if they ever leave; your rock hosts and supports it. The rock's is a work asset. The brain lives in your rock's account and a leaver hands it back. Ownership can move later, with consent on both sides. If you are hosting a community of independent people, you almost always want Theirs.
  3. Who is it for? Their name and their email. The email is load-bearing: only that address can ever sign in to their assistant. It is their private key to the door, and a mistyped address here is the one mistake that costs a rebuild. Everything else (username, email provider, region) sits under Advanced (optional) and works itself out from the address; you rarely need to open it.
  4. Press New pebble. The build runs on its own and shows its steps as it goes. You can leave the page; it finishes without you.

You should see, when it lands: a card headed Done. Now send them their invite, with the message already written and addressed. One press on Open email (or Send on WhatsApp) sends them everything they need: the app download, the link, and what will happen when they open it.

Three things about that invite, because they are the things people ask:

  • It expires in 48 hours. Send it when they can act on it, not on a Friday for a Monday session. An expired link costs nothing: reissue it from their card on the roster and send the fresh one.
  • It only works for the address you typed. A forwarded link will not claim somebody else's mineral. That refusal is deliberate.
  • Their mineral is asleep until they sign Claude in. Nothing runs before they turn the key themselves, which is also why an invitation sitting unopened costs nobody anything.

When their app asks for a code#

Setting up completes on its own: the member's device proves itself during the claim, and it is let in automatically, usually inside two minutes. Nobody approves a device by hand, and that is deliberate: an approve button for something the machine can prove would only train people to press approve.

Sometimes the member's screen shows a 6-character safety code while this happens. That is the door being careful, not a task for you: the code exists so a human can check the right machine is being let in, and setup carries on once the check completes.

A device that cannot prove itself is refused, and refusals land under Devices that could not be let in on your Decisions page, each with the reason and what fixes it. That section is a record rather than a queue: nothing there waits for an approve, and the usual fix is simply a fresh invite.

Watching them arrive#

Every member is a card on the roster, and the card is honest about where they are: Invited (link sent, not yet opened), Setting up (mineral built, still wiring), Active (heartbeats arriving), Quiet (nothing heard for a while, with how long). What each card can tell you about a member's activity is theirs to switch on, not yours to take.

The one number worth watching in the first week is not sign-ups; it is how many finished the interview. A member who never met their assistant has nothing to come back for, and the host guides on your rock are about exactly that.

Approving people who ask to join#

If you list your rock publicly, people can ask. Requests queue on your Decisions page, and you approve each one yourself: there is no setting that admits strangers automatically, and there is not going to be.

Removing someone#

You can end a tie from the Members page (End, on their card). What that does and does not do:

  • It ends the membership: your skills stop arriving, your shared pages stop arriving, they leave your roster.
  • It does not delete their mineral, their brain or their assistant.
  • If they were anchored, hosting stops being yours to pay for. The machine is still theirs.

There is deliberately no host action that destroys a member's mineral. If a member wants their machine gone, they ask us and we destroy it, and that is their decision to make rather than yours.

Handing a mineral over#

Custody can move in both directions, and either direction needs the sign-in of the person receiving it. A community can hand a mineral to the member on it, and a member can accept custody of one that was anchored to a community. Transfer, on the member's card, starts it; nothing moves until the receiving side signs in and accepts, and both the app and Telegram tell the member the moment anything about ownership or access changes.
