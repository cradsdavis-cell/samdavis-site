Questions, answered straight

Short answers, in the order people ask them. Every answer links the page that carries the detail, and nothing here says anything the deeper pages do not.

What actually is this?#

A private AI assistant that lives on a machine of your own (we call the machine a mineral), knows your world because it interviewed you, and works for you on a schedule: inbox sorted before you wake, a brief that knows what matters, follow-ups chased. What an AI EA actually is makes the full argument; rocks and pebbles explains the two words you will keep meeting.

Can you read my stuff?#

No. Your notes live on your machine, not on a central store, and operating the machine does not include reading it. What we can see is operational: whether your mineral is alive and which software it runs. The one exception is a support session you grant, which expires on a clock and is logged on your own mineral: what a grant opens, and the privacy policy in full.

Can my community leader read my stuff?#

No. A rock can send things to your mineral and sees a small status heartbeat, most of which you control with two switches. It cannot read your pages, and there is no page in their app that would show them. What your community can see lists every field.

What does it cost?#

During the beta, nothing is charged: no payment method is taken, and any price you see in the app is indicative. What hosting will cost is settled in the open on 26 September 2026, and nothing converts to paid without your explicit agreement to a stated price. That promise is in the terms of service, not just here.

Do I need to be technical?#

No. If you can install an app and answer questions in plain language, you can run a mineral. Your first hour assumes nothing, and the one technical-looking setup (Google, with your own key) is walked click by click. The deliberately technical route exists too, for those who want it: Claude Code on your mineral.

Do I need a Claude account?#

Yes. Your assistant runs on Claude, on your Claude account, on your machine. That is deliberate: it keeps us out of your conversations. You sign in once during your first hour, and scheduled jobs run on that sign-in.

Where is my data kept?#

Your mineral runs on a rented machine in the EU (Germany or Finland), and your brain also backs up to a private GitHub repository in your own account. If you are in Australia, your data is stored overseas; the privacy policy says so plainly, because Australian privacy law expects it and you should know regardless.

What happens if Crads AI disappears?#

You keep everything. Your brain is ordinary files on a machine you hold, your backup is in your own repository, and the wind-down commitments are contractual, not a statement of intent. What happens if Crads AI goes away answers this at length, starting by conceding it is the right question.

Can I leave? What do I keep?#

Leave any time, keep everything: your brain, your backup, and the mineral itself if you want to take the machine. Ask and we destroy the machine and its records. There is nothing to claw back and no reason required. The terms section 7 is the binding version.

What is the interview, and do I have to do it in one go?#

The interview is the hour that turns a blank machine into an assistant that knows your world, and no, it resumes exactly where you stopped, days later if need be. Three twenty-minute sittings beat one exhausted hour. The interview explains the eight layers and what happens to your answers.

The brief got something wrong. Is that it?#

No, that is day two. Tell your assistant, in plain words, in the Terminal: what it got wrong and what matters instead. It writes that down and the next brief reads it. An assistant you correct gets sharper every week; your first week builds the habit.

Something looks broken. Where do I start?#

When something looks wrong lists the eight symptoms people actually hit, each with its one-line cause and fix. If a scheduled job went quiet, the answer is usually on Connections: jobs fail rather than pretend when a connection dies.

How is this different from just using ChatGPT?#

Ask a chat window "what should I focus on today" and you get advice-shaped filler, because it cannot know you. Your assistant answers from your actual goals, your actual inbox, your actual people, and it acts on a schedule instead of waiting to be asked. The longer answer, including what this is not, is what an AI EA actually is.

Who owns what I write? Do you train on it?#

You own your content; we claim no licence over it and do not train on it. The terms say both in as many words. Your assistant's answers to you run on your own Claude account, under your agreement with Anthropic.
