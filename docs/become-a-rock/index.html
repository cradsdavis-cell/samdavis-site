Turn your mineral into a rock

A rock is a mineral that hosts other minerals. If you run a community, a cohort or a team and you want their assistants to live somewhere you curate, this is the upgrade.

You do it from your own mineral. Nobody approves it. Crads AI does not sit in the middle of this decision, and the app used to imply otherwise in four places before that copy was corrected: a promise of a human who is never coming is worse than no promise, because you wait for it.

The one thing to understand first#

One thing changes hands, and it is the big one: your personal brain becomes the rock's brain. The pages your assistant knows you by become the pages the community runs on, and what your rock later publishes to members draws on them.

That is the right shape for a founder whose working brain is the community's brain. If yours is full of things that are yours alone, the honest move is to start a fresh rock instead (the app's start screen offers New Rock) and keep this mineral as your own.

The upgrade takes this seriously enough that it will not run until you have typed that exact fact back at it, and a verified copy of your brain is pushed to your own repository first, so your pre-rock self is always recoverable.

Before you press it#

Connect GitHub first. The upgrade pushes your brain to your own private repository before it changes anything. If GitHub is not connected, the backup page walks it; connect, then come back.

That ordering is the whole safety story: backup, then upgrade. Not the reverse.

If a rock built your mineral for you, two cases:

  • You are anchored to them (they pay for your hosting): you can still promote. The anchor ends automatically as part of the upgrade, because a rock cannot be anchored to a rock. You stay joined to their community, and what you pay changes: they stop paying for your seat, and you start paying for a rock. The card says all of this before you type anything.
  • They own your mineral (it was built as a work asset): it cannot become a rock until they hand it over to you, because a rock has to own itself. The card says so, and the handover is their act, from their own console.

Step by step#

  1. Open Your pebble and scroll to the Your rock card.
  2. Press Promote to a rock.... The form unfolds in place, as above.
  3. Pick a handle: lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, like acme-co. This becomes the name people join, so choose it the way you would choose a web address. The display name beside it is the human name, and that one you can change later (the rename lives on the Your rock identity card once you are a rock); the handle you cannot. Choose both as public words: handle and display name are readable by anyone the moment they are registered, even while your rock stays private and unlisted, because the directory answers name lookups with them.
  4. Type the consent sentence, exactly: my personal brain becomes this rock's brain. The button stays grey until you do. A checkbox would soften what changes hands here, so there is no checkbox.
  5. Press Back up, then become a rock.

You should see: the card reports each stage as it goes: your backup pushed, the name registered, the mineral changing shape. It takes a minute or two, and you can close the app: it finishes on its own, and the card picks it up again whenever you come back. Your mineral keeps its address, its key and this seat throughout.

What is actually happening#

Three stages, in a deliberate order. First, your box pushes a verified copy of your brain to your own repository, before anything else runs; the card counts the seconds while it does ("Pushing a verified copy to your own repo first. This usually takes under a minute on a busy brain."). Second, the request is handed to Crads AI's machinery, which does the org-side work (registering the name, setting up the rock's account) with its keys on its infrastructure, never with credentials on your box. Third, and only once that work is done, your mineral flips its own shape.

While the middle stage runs, the card says "Registering your rock" and checks again every 15 seconds. If the directory cannot be reached for a moment it says so, and adds: "Nothing is lost: the request is parked and finishes on its own." That resilience is not the browser tab being patient. The watch resumes from a durable marker on your mineral itself, which is exactly what makes "you can close the app" true: come back on any device, days later, and the card reads the marker and picks the watch back up.

One honesty note on "nobody approves it": an automatic billing check can briefly hold a request (at today's $0 pricing everything passes it), so a held promote is a system state with its reason shown on the progress card, not a human deciding.

If it stops partway#

A promotion that fails does not strand you, and it does not guess. The card flips to "This promotion stopped before it finished", shows the reason, and says the thing that matters: "Your mineral is untouched and your backup is still yours." One button, Clear it and start over, puts the form back.

A request that never started at all gets the same sentence, and one refusal is named outright: a handle that belongs to someone else tells you to pick another, while a name you registered yourself carries straight on to finishing the change instead of refusing you your own rock.

When it lands#

The finish line is one sentence on the card: "this mineral is your rock, and your personal seat stays. Reopen the app and the rock face appears beside it." You end up with two faces of the same box: your personal seat, unchanged, and the rock console next to it.

Your pages, your skills and your cadence all survive. You are adding capability, not starting over.

The figure on the Billing card in that screenshot is indicative. Nothing is charged during the beta, no payment method is required, and what hosting will cost gets settled in the open on 26 September. Becoming a rock does not sign you up to anything.

Your maintenance changes today#

The upgrade is also the moment three things stop being automatic: the nightly backup, the nightly software update, and the scheduled refresh that keeps connections signed in. All three are a pebble's own housekeeping, and a rock runs none of them unattended; they become your deliberate acts. Running your rock is the whole of that job, and none of it is daily.

What you get#

The panel grows a set of pages your mineral did not have:

  • Members, the roster of minerals you host.
  • Your rock, where custody, the public listing and hosting sit.
  • Decisions, the queue of things waiting on your answer: join requests and anchor asks.
  • Organisations, other communities yours is tied to.
  • A catalogue, so you can publish skills to your members.
  • The ability to build minerals for people, anchored to you.

The first thing to do as a rock#

Not inviting. Your rock's own interview: the same eight layers, asked about the community instead of about a person. A rock cannot build minerals for members until its own brain is onboarded; the check that gates building reads exactly this state.

Private by default#

A new rock is private: people join by invitation only. You can list it publicly later, from Your rock, and that is a deliberate second decision rather than a default you have to notice and undo.

What being a rock does not give you#

It does not let you read your members' brains. Hosting a mineral is not reading it, and there is no page in the panel that would show you their notes, because their notes are on their machine.

This is worth being clear-eyed about before you invite anybody, because the people you invite will ask, and the answer you want to be able to give is the structural one rather than a reassurance about your own conduct. What you are accountable for as a host, and what stays ours, is set out in the hosting agreement.
