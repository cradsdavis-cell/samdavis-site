How to

Turn your mineral into a rock

A rock is a mineral that hosts other minerals. If you run a community, a cohort or a team and you want their assistants to live somewhere you curate, this is the upgrade.

You do it from your own mineral. Nobody approves it. Crads AI does not sit in the middle of this decision, and the app used to imply otherwise in four places before that copy was corrected: a promise of a human who is never coming is worse than no promise, because you wait for it.

Before you press it

Connect GitHub first. The upgrade pushes your brain to your own private repository before it changes anything, so that if the upgrade goes wrong there is an off-machine copy of everything you had. If GitHub is not connected, connect it and come back.

That ordering is the whole safety story: backup, then upgrade. Not the reverse.

What happens

  1. Your brain is pushed to your own repository.
  2. Your mineral restarts with the rock machinery added.
  3. It registers itself so people can be invited to it.

Your brain, your pages, your skills and your cadence all survive. You are adding capability, not starting over.

The figure on the Billing card in that screenshot is indicative. Nothing is charged during the beta, no payment method is required, and what hosting will cost gets settled in the open on 26 September. Becoming a rock does not sign you up to anything.

What you get

The panel grows a set of pages your mineral did not have:

  • Members, the roster of minerals you host.
  • Your rock, where custody, the public listing and hosting sit.
  • Rocks, other communities yours is tied to.
  • A catalogue, so you can publish skills to your members.
  • The ability to build minerals for people, anchored to you.

Private by default

A new rock is private: people join by invitation only. You can list it publicly later, from Your rock, and that is a deliberate second decision rather than a default you have to notice and undo.

What being a rock does not give you

It does not let you read your members' brains. Hosting a mineral is not reading it, and there is no page in the panel that would show you their notes, because their notes are on their machine.

This is worth being clear-eyed about before you invite anybody, because the people you invite will ask, and the answer you want to be able to give is the structural one rather than a reassurance about your own conduct. What you are accountable for as a host, and what stays ours, is set out in the hosting agreement.
