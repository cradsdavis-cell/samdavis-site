What an AI EA actually is

There is a lot of noise around AI assistants, and most of it describes a chat window. This is not that, so it is worth saying carefully what it is.

An AI executive assistant is an assistant that lives in your working life rather than in a tab. It holds a real, readable model of your world (your goals, your people, your commitments, your voice) and it acts on a schedule as well as on request. The difference is not intelligence. It is context and residence.

The test that separates it from a chatbot#

Ask a chat window "what should I focus on today?" and you get advice-shaped filler, because it does not know you. It cannot: you arrived through a login page thirty seconds ago.

Ask an assistant that holds your goals, reads your calendar and your inbox, and knows which three commitments are slipping, and the same question has an actual answer. That is the entire category difference in one question. Everything else follows from it.

What changes about a week, concretely#

Not magic. A handful of small, compounding removals:

  • You stop opening your inbox to decide things. Triage happened at seven, before you were up.
  • The day starts with three priorities picked against your actual goals, not eleven calendar entries: the daily brief.
  • The threads where somebody owes you a reply stop silently dying: the follow-up sweep.
  • The week gets planned on Monday and reviewed on Sunday, against the same written outcomes.

Each is minor. Together they are the difference between running your week and your week running you, and none of them requires you to remember to ask. Each arrives as a recipe in the library on your mineral, with its own guide beside it; the Skills page is where they land.

Who this is actually for#

A business owner is the centre of the target. One person holding customers, suppliers, pricing, a pipeline and a family in their head, with no assistant because assistants cost a salary. The interview moves that world out of their head into pages an assistant can act on: how.

A founder is the same shape earlier: more moving parts than hours, relationships that die of silence, and a calendar that eats the strategy time.

A community or an organisation is where it gets interesting, and it is what the rock model exists for. A coaching practice, a cohort, an association: the leader curates and vouches, each member gets their own private assistant, and the community's shared context (its language, its frameworks, its playbook) reaches every member's assistant without anyone's private material being pooled. The leader can see who has gone quiet, unless a member has switched that sharing off, and cannot see inside anything.

What it is not#

Not a virtual assistant. A VA brings judgement and hands; this brings memory and cadence. A VA can negotiate with your landlord. This can make sure you never again forget that the negotiation is due, draft the email in your voice, and hold the history. Plenty of people need both.

Not an automation platform. Tools like n8n and Zapier move data between apps along rails you build, and they are good at it. This is the other end: you do not build rails, you have a conversation, and the judgement calls stay visible. When something should be a hard rail, a scheduled skill is one, but the product does not make you think in flowcharts.

Not a chatbot with your logo. The empty version of this product is exactly that, which is why the interview is treated as the product and not as onboarding friction.

The honest prerequisites#

It needs an hour of genuine interview before it is any good. It needs connections to your real accounts, with the Google one costing ten minutes because we chose to keep your key out of our hands. And it repays attention: an assistant you correct gets sharper, one you only consume from plateaus.

If that trade sounds right, your first hour is where it starts. If you run a community and want this for your members, hosting a rock is yours. And if you came with one specific question, the FAQ answers the usual ones straight.
