## 1. Authority position

> **AI is moving so fast that people are getting left behind. I catch them up.
> Fax machine to mobile phone.**

Use this as the spine. It came out of a positioning session with Jemimah Ashleigh in July 2026
and it beats the earlier framing ("help people surf the wave", "meet them where they are"),
which sounded gentle but signalled low ambition. "Catching them up" names a destination.

**The problem he solves:** business owners who are not technical are falling behind on AI
because the pace of change outstrips the pace of learning.

**Who for:** founders, small-business owners and independent professionals, not engineers.

**What is different:** he is a teacher first. Clients walk away owning the thing and the skill
to keep growing it, rather than renting a black box.

## 2. Bios

### Short (25 words)
Sam Davis teaches non-technical business owners to build an AI assistant that actually knows
them: their voice, their people, their week. He built his first.

### Medium (100 words)
Sam Davis is a Welsh AI coach and systems builder based in Sydney. He built an AI executive
assistant that runs his own week, drafting his email, tracking every client and flagging what
is slipping, then started teaching business owners to build their own. None of them were
technical, which is the point. Before this he spent three and a half years on a PhD in
Bayesian environmental data science at the University of Sydney, engineered data pipelines at
UWA, ran a ten-person AI sprint at the Alan Turing Institute, and worked on satellite
propulsion at the European Space Agency. He is a teacher before he is a technologist.

### Long (250 words)
Sam Davis is an AI coach, systems builder and translator based in Sydney.

He grew up Welsh-speaking in Blaenau Ffestiniog, in the heart of Eryri, and went through a
Welsh-medium state comprehensive before a gap year teaching at Harrow International School in
Bangkok at eighteen. He read Physics and Mathematics at the University of Manchester, finishing
with First Class honours and placing fourth in his cohort, then spent a summer at the European
Space Agency working out the propellant budget for the FLEX satellite by teaching himself
orbital mechanics. That pattern, being handed a problem he does not yet know how to solve and
building the framework that solves the whole class of it, has repeated ever since.

His PhD at the University of Sydney's DARE ARC Training Centre produced BayeSpace, a Bayesian
statistical framework applied to atmospheric dispersion and species distribution modelling,
along with two novel empirical findings. He has facilitated a ten-person AI sprint at the Alan
Turing Institute, architected a three-tier environmental data warehouse at the University of
Western Australia, and tutored engineering mechanics at Sydney for three years.

Since 2025 he has built eight AI systems in twelve months and coached business owners to build
their own AI executive assistants. He is also a co-founder of Wildly Calm, which runs men's
mental-health retreats in the Australian bush, and was part of the British Alpine Club team
that made the first ascent of Pik Perseverance, a 4,788m virgin peak in Kyrgyzstan, in 2021.

He lives in Coogee, in a van called Nola.

## 3. Logo and marks

**Primary mark:** a dark green profile silhouette holding out an open palm, with a circuit-tree
motif of gold nodes and green leaves rising above it. It carries the whole positioning in one
image: a person, offering, technology growing rather than technology imposed.

| File | Size | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `assets/mark-primary.png` | 1200 x 1200 | Master. Any print or large digital use. |
| `assets/mark-primary-transparent.png` | 1200 x 1200 | Master on transparent background, for placing on colour. |
| `assets/mark-portrait.png` | 520 x 864 | Portrait crop as used on the site. |
| `assets/favicon.png` | 64 x 64 | Favicon and tiny placements. |

**Clear space:** leave at least 10% of the mark's width clear on every side.
**Do not:** recolour the mark, stretch it, add effects, or place it on a busy photograph.

**Gap, flagged honestly:** there is no vector (SVG or EPS) version and no wordmark. Print
publications sometimes ask for vector. See § 8.

## 8. Story angles for media

Ranked by how well they travel.

1. **Fax machine to mobile phone.** The pace argument. AI is not scary, it is fast, and the
   people getting hurt are the ones nobody is teaching. Sits well in small-business press.
2. **He built an AI that runs his own business, then taught his clients to build theirs.**
   The live-demo angle. His whole practice runs on the system he sells.
3. **Welsh-medium comprehensive to the European Space Agency.** Blaenau Ffestiniog, a state
   school he has called "not a very good school", to Harrow Bangkok at eighteen, Manchester,
   ESA at twenty-two, a PhD in Sydney. Strong for education and social-mobility desks.
4. **The van.** He lives in a 2014 Renault Master campervan called Nola in Coogee while running
   an AI practice. Good lifestyle and future-of-work hook. *Sam's call whether this is on.*
5. **First ascent of a virgin peak.** Kyrgyzstan 2021, Pik Perseverance, 4,788m, as part of a
   five-climber British Alpine Club team. Adventure and profile pieces.
6. **Bayesian statistics for the environment.** BayeSpace, and two novel findings: oceanic
   atmospheric dispersion may be more intense than terrestrial models predict, and there is a
   20% grid-coverage threshold below which species distribution data stops being usable.
   Science and climate desks.
