## 2. The ideas Sam wants to be asked about

This is the section for producers and hosts. These are the things he will talk well about for
an hour, in rough order of how much he wants the question.

### 2.1 Pam ddim y ddau, and going to the edges to land in the middle

*Pam ddim y ddau* is Welsh for **"why not both?"** It is the closest thing Sam has to a
personal philosophy, and it is not a compromise strategy. The point is not to find a
comfortable middle. It is to go **fully** into both ends of something, hold the tension, and
build the capacity to live with the range.

He first articulated it after a tiny-home building course at the Centre for Alternative
Technology, surrounded by two groups who did not overlap: entrepreneurs and impact-makers in
one column, community-dwellers and sustainability people in the other. His conclusion:

> You can be a CEO and live in a tiny home. You can grow your own veggies and still be
> passionate about AI. Life in one bubble is boring. I'd rather be continuously challenged to
> find the balance than settle on one extreme.

The version he uses now: **look to the edges, so your conclusions land in the middle.** Not
splitting the difference. Going out to both extremes deliberately, and letting the honest
answer emerge from having actually been to both. There is something of his Bayesian training
in this, the discipline of holding a wide distribution before collapsing to an estimate, and
it is worth asking him to draw the connection.

The claim underneath it is contrarian and worth pushing on: **most apparent trade-offs are not
trade-offs.** Rigour or warmth. Ambition or peace. Technology or nature. He will argue that in
almost every case the choice is a failure of imagination, and that the discomfort of holding
both is not the price of growth but the mechanism of it.

**Good questions:** What did you have to give up to hold both? When has this been wrong?
Where are you still just telling yourself a nice story?

### 2.2 Wild and calm, and why the two need each other

Sam co-founded **Wildly Calm**, which runs men's mental-health retreats in the Australian bush.
The premise is that adventure and mindfulness are **one practice, not two**. You take a group
into something genuinely hard, you build safety inside the discomfort rather than removing the
discomfort, you expand, you integrate. Physical edge and inner stillness in the same weekend,
on purpose.

This is not a side interest, it is the same philosophy in physical form. The person who
first-ascended a 4,788m peak in Kyrgyzstan is the same person who has sat multiple silent
meditation retreats and meditates daily. He is drawn hard in both directions and treats the
oscillation as a signal rather than a problem: it is how he tells whether an environment is
right, because the ones that only offer one pole always feel incomplete.

The angle a good host can get at: **men's mental health handled without the usual register.**
No performative masculinity, no corporate wellness, no bro-ey stuff. Those are explicit
exclusions in Wildly Calm's own mission statement. What is left is harder and more interesting.

**A live hook, not a deadline.** Sam is Project Lead for the Snowies retreat on 3 to 6
September, so there is a real thing happening on a date, which is useful for a pitch. He
remains a co-founder afterwards; Lockie and Kieran lead the October and November retreats.

### 2.3 AI, ethics, and what actually happens next

**This is the territory Sam most wants to be booked for**, and it is the least represented in
his public footprint so far.

He is not an academic ethicist and the kit should not pretend otherwise. What he is: a
physicist who spent three and a half years doing Bayesian uncertainty modelling, then spent
twelve months building production AI systems, and who now spends his working life watching
non-technical people meet this technology for the first time. That combination is rarer on a
podcast than another ethicist, and it is the reason to have him on.

Three threads he can carry:

**The maternal-instinct alignment thesis.** In the animal kingdom there is essentially no case
of a more intelligent animal choosing, at real cost to itself, to act in the interest of a less
intelligent one. The exception is mothers and their offspring. So if we want a superintelligence
to act in the interests of less-intelligent humans even when it costs it something, the
maternal bond is the one natural template we have for benevolent power-asymmetry, and it is
what we should be trying to instil. It is a genuine, arguable position, it is his own, and it
survives being pushed on. It also invites good counter-argument, which makes it a strong
discussion prompt rather than a monologue.

**Timelines as an agency device, not a doom device.** Sam's read is that insiders expect this
faster than they say publicly, and that the honest response to a short timeline is not despair
but repositioning. He is deliberately not a doomer. He is also not a booster. The talk he wants
to give is "here is what is coming and here is how you move", and he is sharp on why the
doom framing actively disempowers the people who most need to act.

**Uncertainty as a discipline.** His doctorate was in Bayesian statistics, which is the
mathematics of holding beliefs proportional to evidence and updating them properly. He will
argue that almost every bad public conversation about AI, in both directions, is a failure to
do this. That is a genuinely distinctive lens and nobody else on the circuit is bringing it.

**Solarpunk.** The optimistic counterweight to cyberpunk: a future where technology and the
living world are integrated rather than opposed, decentralised rather than concentrated, and
where the aesthetic of progress is green rather than chrome. Sam's whole life argues for this
and so does his logo, which is a leaf whose veins are circuit traces. Worth knowing that the
visual identity and the philosophy are the same idea.

**Register reference.** If it helps to calibrate: the conversation Sam wants is roughly the one
**Mo Gawdat** has on *Diary of a CEO*. A technologist who left the industry's inside and now
talks about values, timelines and what ordinary people should do, for a general audience, and
who lands somewhere hopeful without being naive. Same register, different expertise: Gawdat
brings the ex-Google-executive vantage, Sam brings uncertainty mathematics and the view from
actually teaching this to people who are frightened of it.

**Good questions:** What do the builders know that the public does not? Is alignment a
technical problem or a parenting problem? What should someone who is not technical do this
year? Why are you not a doomer?

## 7. Getting it right

Five things that are easy to get wrong and matter to Sam.

1. **Kyrgyzstan: team member, not leader.** He was one of five on the British Alpine Club team
   that made the first ascent. He did not lead the expedition. Please never write that he did.
2. **He is not an AI ethicist, and would not claim to be.** He is a builder and a physicist with
   a considered view. Frame the § 2.3 material as an informed practitioner's argument, not as
   academic or professional expertise in ethics. He is more interesting that way and it is also
   the truth.
3. **The thesis findings are not published or peer-reviewed.** They are findings from his
   doctoral thesis. *"His PhD thesis found..."* is fine. *"Research shows..."* is not. There is
   no paper and no DOI to link.
4. **British citizen.** Not Australian. He lives in Australia on a visa.
5. **Seven builds, not eight.** An older count of eight is still floating about in his own
   material. Seven is correct.

## 8. The logo

**The mark is the circuit-leaf.** A leaf drawn in deep green whose veins are circuit traces,
each ending in a via-ring, with one node filled terracotta. It is the positioning in one shape:
something growing and something engineered, the same object. It is also, deliberately, the
solarpunk argument in § 2.3 rendered as a logo.

It is drawn from geometry rather than stored as artwork, so it is genuinely resolution-free.
Vector is supplied.

| File | Format | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `assets/leaf-mark.svg` | Vector | **Default.** Badged: leaf on a cream rounded square. |
| `assets/leaf-mark-1024.png` | 1024 x 1024 | Raster master of the badged mark. |
| `assets/leaf-mark-512.png` | 512 x 512 | Raster, common web and profile size. |
| `assets/leaf-mark-64.png` | 64 x 64 | Favicon and tiny placements. |
| `assets/leaf-mark-on-light.svg` / `.png` | Vector + 1024 | Badge removed, green leaf, transparent. **Light backgrounds only.** |
| `assets/leaf-mark-reversed.svg` / `.png` | Vector + 1024 | Cream leaf, transparent. **For dark backgrounds.** |

**Pick the right one:** badged by default. On a light surface where the badge would look boxy,
use `on-light`. On anything dark, use `reversed`. The green leaf disappears against a dark
background, so never use `on-light` there.

**Clear space:** at least 10% of the mark's width on every side.
**Do not:** recolour beyond the two supplied variants, stretch, add effects, or place on a busy
photograph.
